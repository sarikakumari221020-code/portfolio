01Overview

Protecting the people most often left unprotected.

The project began by studying how house-breaking crimes and emergencies disproportionately affect elderly people living alone. Through primary and secondary research we found a design space in door-interaction verification, simple community alerts, and everyday preventive habits — and a strong pull toward safety accessories that are always within reach.

02The Problem

Burglars exploit opportunity, not technology.

Most intrusions happen through simple forced entry or by bluffing as delivery staff — and most occur in daylight, when homes appear unoccupied. Isolation, predictable routines and weak neighbourhood connections turn elderly and women living alone into easy targets.

03Research

A dataset, then real doorsteps.

Secondary research filtered 1,438 vulnerable-group cases from a 1,980-case burglary dataset (2020–2024), supported by NCRB and HelpAge India reports. Primary interviews with police, women and elderly residents grounded the numbers in lived experience.

"Most cases occur when the house appears unoccupied or predictable routines are noticed."

— Police personnel

"Delivery people make me slightly uneasy — especially when I'm alone."

— Woman, living alone

"I don't fully understand how to use the door camera my son installed."

— Elderly resident
04Insights

Fear, low tech-confidence, and lonely verification.

High fear at the door

Elderly users hesitate to answer when they can't recognise a visitor, and rely on guards or neighbours to validate.

Low confidence with tech

CCTV and smart locks provide emotional reassurance but are rarely reviewed or fully understood.

Neighbours over police

Women rely more on neighbours than police for immediate safety in the critical first moments.

The design shift

Users wanted something always accessible, non-stigmatising and effortless — which moved us from apps toward wearable accessories.

05Ideation

Why a wearable — and why a ring & pendant.

Always-on

Emergencies are sudden. Unlike a phone, an accessory is worn 24×7 — safety literally on the body.

One-tap

No menus or screens — even a digitally non-literate user can ask for help instantly.

No stigma

It looks like jewellery, not a medical gadget — protecting dignity and comfort.

Culturally familiar

Rings and pendants are already part of daily routines, so they're easy to remember and wear.

Across all variations, the ring and pendant offered the best balance of accessibility, comfort, emotional acceptance and all-day wearability.

07Reflection

Support, not surveillance.

SafeTap doesn't aim to control elderly lives — it supports them quietly, giving the reassurance that help is always within reach. The biggest lesson: for this audience, emotional acceptance is a functional requirement. A device that works but feels stigmatising simply won't be worn.
