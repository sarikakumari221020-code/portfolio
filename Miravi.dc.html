01The Brief

Design for a self-critical generation.

Miravi is built for the high-functioning perfectionist between 18 and 30 — the person who isn't clinically anxious, but carries a daily voice saying "I'm not good enough." Students, young workers and creatives who feel they must never make a mistake. They don't need a doctor — a small, kind daily tool can genuinely help.

02The Problem

The blocks getting in the way.

The process began with a hand-written letter to an imaginary inner guide — the playful, brave part of the self that creates without fear. Then a visual map named the internal blocks: overthinking, self-doubt, judgement, fear, comparison, low confidence, procrastination — the inner critic.

04The Pivot

The right context. The wrong medium.

Rejected — The Flush

A phone app: type the mean thought, watch it flush away. Naming an unkind thought is the first CBT step — but it was still an app, living in the same phone where the comparison started. Instagram one swipe away.

Chosen — The Mirror

The bathroom was right; the medium was wrong. Mirror engagement is daily and unavoidable — 78% use their phones in the washroom. Miravi simply replaces a hostile screen with a kind one.

05Naming

Three names rejected. Each sharpened the brief.

ReflectAINamed the technology, not the feeling. The user doesn't want to interact with AI — they want to interact with themselves, gently.
InneraNamed the destination, not the act. The user isn't arriving — they're pausing.
miravi ✓mira (Latin: to look, to wonder) + vi (life, energy). The mirror is not a passive surface — it looks back.

08Reflection

"The mirror is not the problem. The way we talk to ourselves in front of it is."

Miravi taught me that the hardest design decision is often where a product should live, not how it looks. Moving from a phone app to a mirror changed everything — the context did the emotional work the interface couldn't.
