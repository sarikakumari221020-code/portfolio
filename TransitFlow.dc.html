01Overview & Outcomes

The problem, the approach, the results.

The problem

Dehradun's transport is dominated by informal modes — Vikrams, shared minivans, e-rickshaws — that no mainstream navigation app covers. For a newcomer, every journey is a series of unverifiable decisions. The result: overcharging, hours lost waiting, and a permanent switch to cabs at 3–10× the cost.

The reframe

Nine interviews showed users weren't short of information — they were short of confidence. Every workaround they'd invented was a substitute for a confirmation signal the system never gave. A confidence gap, not an information gap — that reframe became the spine of the project.

03Research & Problem Definition

Nine people, ages 20 to 50, on the same broken routes.

Participants were deliberately spread across newcomers and long-term locals, so "confident" behaviour could be compared directly against "lost" behaviour on the same routes. Semi-structured interviews were paired with secondary transport data, then synthesised through affinity and journey mapping.

Four findings from primary research

Finding 017/9

No reliable route information

Locals give conflicting directions — asking more people makes the picture worse, not better.

Finding 02

Unpredictable waiting

Shared vehicles leave only once full, so departures are never predictable. Roodren waited 2+ hours for a bus that never came.

Finding 035/9

Fare exploitation

₹50 → ₹150 for ISBT to GMS Road — a 3× markup on a standard route, charged to outsiders.

Finding 044/6

The cab-switching fallback

Newcomers abandoned PT mid-journey — a direct financial penalty for an information gap.

04Synthesis — The Confidence Gap

The same interviews, read for behaviour instead of complaints.

Surface reading

"No route info, no timings, no fare transparency." Generic — every city produces this report. Not a differentiator.

The deeper pattern

Users don't trust the system. They depend on other humans to verify decisions, and constantly doubt choices they've already made. Cab-switching is a loss of confidence, not a lack of route information.

Five behavioural clusters

Confirmation

The Verification Loop

Users want confirmation, not information. Each answer should resolve doubt; instead it multiplies it.

Uncertainty

The Paralysis Wait

No ETA means no decision. Users freeze until urgency forces the cab.

Trust

The Fare Ambush

With no reference to challenge a fare, an overcharge becomes a trust-destruction event.

Decision collapse

The Stress Exit

Switching to a cab is a surrender. When confidence collapses under time pressure, users exit — and don't return.

Blind journey

The Silent Disruption

Even an experienced local got zero warning about a route blockage until arrival.

The model

The cab exit happens here

Between boarding and the decision point, the user receives no signal of any kind. That silent gap is where confidence collapses.

06Wireframes

Deciding the skeleton before any pixel.

[ REPLACE — hand-drawn low-fi wireframe scans ] Welcome · Home · Route entry · Route detail · Boarding guide · Fare checker · Lost-recovery

Decided at low fidelity

  • A persistent five-item bottom nav — "I'm Lost" is never more than one tap away
  • Fare as a first-class element on every route screen
  • A binary WAIT / SWITCH pair — one decision per screen

Deliberately left out

  • A map-first home — maps answer "where", users needed "am I right?"
  • Account creation before first use — friction at the moment trust is lowest
  • Dense timetables — completeness at the cost of confidence

What held up
  • The "correct vehicle" confirmation was the most-praised moment
  • Fare-lock removed pre-boarding anxiety instantly

What broke
  • Route-number entry confused low-literacy users
  • The WAIT / SWITCH labels needed stronger visual weight
  • Disruption alerts arrived without a clear next action for some

10Reflection & Next Steps

What I learned reframing a "map" problem.

What worked

Re-coding interviews for the type of confidence failure rather than the surface complaint turned a generic transport brief into a specific, defensible design direction — and gave every screen a traceable reason to exist.

What I'd change

I'd test with lower-literacy users earlier — Nitu (48) couldn't use any digital tool independently, and that constraint deserved to shape the IA from Phase 1, not surface in Phase 5. Round 2 focuses on a voice-and-icon-first onboarding.
